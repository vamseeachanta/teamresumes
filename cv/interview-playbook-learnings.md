# Executive Interview Playbook — distilled learnings

> Distilled 2026-09-22 from archived interview preparation notes. Fully anonymized:
> no names, companies, emails, phone numbers, or client-identifying details.

## The 10-step preparation method

1. **Career stories** — speak with former colleagues; collect raw material before writing.
2. **Learn the leadership principles cold** — study the hiring company's LPs to the "Nth degree";
   they are the DNA of the interview and the scoring rubric.
3. **Map LPs to stories** — decide which LP each story demonstrates.
4. **Re-read the job description** — anchor stories to the actual role.
5. **Match key LPs with career stories** — every LP the role demands needs at least one story.
6. **Find the gaps** — LPs with no story get one built or borrowed from adjacent experience.
7. **Draft in STARI** — Situation, Task, Action, Result, Insight (the "I" is what most candidates skip).
8. **Edit ruthlessly** — behavioral, not contextual. Cut background; keep decisions and outcomes.
9. **Prepare the opener** — introductory Q&A ("tell us about yourself") gets written answers, not improv.
10. **Practice out loud** — full run-throughs, not silent re-reads.

## Interview mechanics

- **Write down the question** before answering — costs nothing, keeps you focused on the keywords
  you must address.
- **Right level of detail** — enough to show judgment, not so much the panel gets lost.
- **Write the story, prepare for the follow-up** — every claim in a story will be probed one level
  deeper; pre-write the second level too.

## STAR archetypes that work (anonymized)

### Crisis build
- *Situation:* Industry standstill after a major offshore blowout; drilling halted; objectives changing daily.
- *Task:* Containment system needed in weeks, not the usual multi-year cycle.
- *Action:* Treated information as Lego bricks — small documented steps in a versioned timeline;
  sourced all available raw material and components from fabrication yards; engineered a riser
  system from off-the-shelf components onto two available vessels.
- *Result:* System built and installed in 57 days vs. a 2–3 year norm.
- *LPs shown:* Bias for action, Invent & Simplify, Deliver Results.

### Automation win
- *Situation:* Near-real-time drilling applications hampered by a manual data-gathering process;
  algorithm latency frustrated users; the team believed automation was impossible.
- *Task:* Eliminate the manual process without breaking data fidelity.
- *Action:* Went to the data vendor, mapped the actual process end-to-end, found an automatable
  path with a few non-critical assumptions; proposed the data flowcharts and implementation stories.
- *Result:* Near-real-time operation achieved; user traffic tripled by word of mouth; average drill
  time fell from ~90 days to under 30 over three years.
- *LPs shown:* Dive Deep, Earn Trust, Deliver Results.

### Failure recovery
- *Situation:* Quoted a sensor solution for a hard measurement problem without doing the fundamental
  analysis first; the physics didn't support the promise; a valuable customer relationship at risk.
- *Task:* Recover trust and solve the actual problem.
- *Action:* Openly admitted the flawed initial assumption; explained the engineering honestly;
  delivered what *was* achievable (measurement range) so the customer could draw valid conclusions;
  published formal datasheets with features *and limitations*; fed the lesson into a shared
  lessons-learned database; offered a full refund.
- *Result:* Customer declined the refund, satisfied with the diligence and honesty.
- *LPs shown:* Ownership, Customer Obsession, Learn and Be Curious.

### Why-leaving narrative
Frame every departure as a completed chapter: what was built/finished, what changed in the
environment (funding climate, industry cycle), and what the move is *toward* — never just away from.

### Compensation question
"Too early to discuss — I expect compensation commensurate with the role. I'm optimizing for the
challenge and the chance to grow." Deflect once, politely, without naming a number early.

## Questions to ask them

**For the recruiter:** tell me about the company; who is the hiring manager / reporting line;
how did this role come to exist; how did you come across my profile.
**For the hiring manager:** which leadership principles do you personally relate to most
(reveals the real culture behind the posted values).

## Recruiter outreach pattern (LinkedIn)

Short, specific, human: one line of relevant domain credibility ("15 years in X from cradle to
grave"), one line stating the target ("seeking a corporate role in Y"), one clear ask for help,
warm close. Under 100 words. Personalize the first line per recipient.

## Reference leadership-principle set (generic)

Customer Obsession · Ownership · Invent and Simplify · Are Right, A Lot · Learn and Be Curious ·
Hire and Develop the Best · Insist on Highest Standards · Think Big · Bias for Action ·
Frugality · Earn Trust · Dive Deep · Have Backbone (disagree and commit) · Deliver Results.
